# G5: a yellow box on Crescent Street x Eastgate Lane (JR-T-013c)

**Owner's report.** The yellow box tool (Ctrl+Y) would not box Crescent Street x Eastgate Lane (shot 060168e0). The 0.4.6
log refused node 551713 twice with "no box shape can be built here: ... corner arm 3 - arm 1 still crosses the kerb or a
marking margin: no allowed shift clears it". Node 258813 was refused with the same message for a different corner.

**Which junction.** The 0.4.6 log does not give positions for refusals. The Carto export (14 Nov) has only one node where
Eastgate Lane meets Crescent Street. It is at about (-518, 546), with four arms: Crescent Street 16 m on both sides (one-way
westbound, with kerbside parking) and Eastgate Lane, an 8 m Alley, to the north and south. The owner clicked 551713
between the boxes added at (-767, 535) and (-676, 541) and the exit boxes at (-457/-493, 546), so 551713 is this junction.
The other junction (258813) is next to roundabout 258815, but the log has no position for it.

**Why the tool refused (the refusal was wrong).**
- A box side that joins two mouths at a corner is a straight chord across the kerb's curved corner. A chord across a curve
  always runs over the pavement, so until now a corner only cleared when both mouths slid forward until they met on the kerb.
- At this junction, cars turning between Crescent and the alley cross each other just inside the alley mouth, beyond
  Crescent's kerb line. Those crossing points must stay inside the box, so the alley mouth cannot slide forward far enough.
  The corner never closed, and the whole junction got no box.
- A box that keeps all four owner rules does exist here: carriageway only, straight sides, 0.5 m from markings, every
  conflict point inside. The fixture reproduces the exact 0.4.6 refusal for 18 versions of the junction (alley skew,
  width and kerb radius varied). Without the turning paths, 0.4.6 built a box for every one of them.

**What changed (0.4.7, Core/StraightBox.cs).**
- A corner that cannot close by sliding may now be cut by ONE straight chamfer. The chamfer touches the kerb curve but
  never crosses it. Each end stays within the half of its mouth side next to that corner, it keeps the marking margin,
  and every conflict point stays on the box side. Of the lines that fit, the one that cuts off the least area is used.
- The 0.4.6 search still runs first and unchanged. Chamfers are tried only when it leaves a corner that cannot clear. So
  every junction and exit that has a box keeps exactly the same shape and solve time. Tests check this on Moonflower,
  Lindbergh and five other test junctions with chamfers on and off.
- A real refusal still happens when no chamfer fits. It now says why, for example "... no allowed shift clears it and no
  straight cut fits (a cut clear of the kerb would leave 2 conflict point(s) outside)".
- In the test version of this junction, the box has 6 corners with 2 chamfers (1.0 m and 2.1 m in from the kerb curve).
  Solving it takes about 20-50 ms in a managed test run, only when the box is rebuilt.

**Log lines (YellowBoxToolSystem).** Every "yellow box tool:" line now names the target with its position and what meets
there, for example: `node Entity(551713:1) at (-517.98, 546.00), arms: 1 Crescent Street (Two-Lane One-Way Road, 16.0 m),
2 Eastgate Lane (Alley, 8.0 m), ...`. The arm numbers are the ones a failure uses ("corner arm 3 - arm 1"). Exit lines end
with `at (x, z) on <street> (<prefab>, <width> m)`. A no-shape WARN line also carries the solver's notes (footways, which
arms yield, conflict points, shifts). A box with a chamfer is logged as "corner arm N - arm M cut straight D m inside the
kerb curve (chamfer ...)" in the paint log's notes.

**Settings.** None new.
